## Every small milestone is worth celebrating

### Product milestones

Last week I shared a few product milestones in quick succession. One was expected; another wasn't.

The expected one was **[passing 20,000 cumulative downloads](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/avE8zD5SXNnUDkxw3MI2Wg "ColorWander passes 20,000 cumulative downloads")**. I'd already estimated when it would happen, so I had the social media assets ready well in advance.

![A poster celebrating ColorWander passing 20,000 downloads](/images/posts/indie-maker-w31-milestone-20k.jpg)

Another milestone was **[breaking into the top 50 in its category in the Hong Kong App Store download rankings](https://mp.weixin.qq.com/s/w_pxhqBDxILTkiY0HDos-g "ColorWander enters the Hong Kong App Store category top 50")**. The app had already made the top 50 in Macao, Taiwan, and some other regions. I think mainland China was hovering around number 50, but I can't remember exactly. See? This is why you have to keep sharing and celebrating. Otherwise, you really do forget.

![ColorWander at number 44 in the Hong Kong App Store's Photo & Video category](/images/posts/indie-maker-w31-hk-rank.jpg)

There was one more I didn't share separately: my views on an overseas social media platform passed 200,000. When I first started, I used to rack my brain over why nobody was seeing my posts. Looking at things today, it feels a little different. Keep showing up, and keep doing things that help other people.

![212,000 views on an overseas social media platform over the past 30 days, July 31 to August 29](/images/posts/indie-maker-w31-views.jpg)

Beyond all of that, I think **celebrating your milestones as they happen helps the next one come sooner. It creates a positive feedback loop, like stepping on one foot with the other to keep climbing higher**.

## Cooking, running, and podcasts

### Listening to podcasts while cooking

What has changed since I started working from home? One obvious change is that I have more opportunities and time to cook.

After long stretches at my computer, I treat housework and cooking as a chance to rest and relax. Listening to a podcast while chopping vegetables and stir-frying makes it easy to get into a flow state.

![An August calendar card made with ColorWander: wavy altocumulus clouds on August 26](/images/posts/indie-maker-w31-calendar-cloud.jpg)

When I had an office job, I usually listened to podcasts on my commute. This week I listened to all sorts of shows. Which episode stayed with me most? Probably ["E10. Zhang Jinjian: Living Life on Your Own Terms in the AI Era"](https://www.xiaoyuzhoufm.com/episode/6970817fef1cf272a79ee3b1 "Signal and Noise E10. Zhang Jinjian: Living Life on Your Own Terms in the AI Era").

![Signal and Noise E10. Zhang Jinjian: Living Life on Your Own Terms in the AI Era](/images/posts/indie-maker-w31-podcast-signal.jpg)


Why this one? It's an episode from more than six months ago, and I think I listened to it back in January too. This time I noticed that it was published on the very day I started my one-person company. Listening again now, it hits differently.

**What kind of life do you want to live in the AI era?**

I vaguely remember planning to write a six-month review of my one-person company back in July, with that exact title. I never finished it... Hopefully I'll get a chance to finish and share it.

## A few observations from a hackathon

While reviewing last week's work, I realized that some observations from the hackathon a couple of days earlier deserved a post of their own. So I wrote one. If you often take part in hackathons or are interested in AI, [this post](/en/posts/vibehacks-judge-notes) might be worth a read.

I received some questions after publishing it. One from @duqk was a good one, so I'll add it here:

> **Q: Compared with the hackathons you took part in six months ago, how have the participants and projects changed?**
>
> A: Overall, I don't think they've changed that much. One difference was that quite a few people in this competition delivered their final product as a Skill. The format has changed a bit, but the things I wrote about in the post don't change much.
>
> Also, hackathon results involve quite a bit of chance. So if you're competing, what matters is working with people you admire on a genuinely interesting project, whether that's the technology, the product idea, or solving a real need. The rest isn't that important.

## A plan for reading and better sleep

Since throwing myself into AI at the end of 2022, I've gradually found it harder and harder to read books for long stretches and in a sustained way. AI gives you results and feedback so quickly.

But this week, I started trying to get back into the habit. How?

First, some background. Ever since the World Cup in June, I've kept my "World Cup sleep schedule." Without a commute or a clock-in time, my sleep routine has become a bit of a mess. Every night I feel full of energy, but I can't get up early the next morning.

I've often felt I needed to change something. Then I noticed that whenever I put aside the urge to "keep directing the AI" or "just assign this one last task before bed" a little earlier and picked up a book on WeRead instead, I could fall asleep pretty quickly.

So last Thursday I joined WeRead's 365-day reading check-in program and started a WeRead group on WeChat, where we can hold each other accountable. If you're interested in reading too, feel free to get in touch and join. We can read and check in together.

![Reading group announcement: Even in the AI era, remember to read for 15 minutes every day](/images/posts/indie-maker-w31-reading-group.png)
